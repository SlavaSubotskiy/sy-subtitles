Preferences menu

One home for the settings that follow the user across every view. The trigger (.prefs-trigger) sits in the header row; the panel (.prefs-menu) is anchored to it and right-aligned, since it opens at the right edge of the window. It is shown open here — in the app it carries .open only while up, and Escape or a click outside dismisses it.

Two kinds of row. A segmented choice (.prefs-seg) puts every option on screen, so the user picks rather than clicking one button until the right value comes round; the current one is marked with aria-pressed="true", which is also what styles it. A switch (.prefs-switch) is a real <input type="checkbox">, tinted but drawn by the browser — a hand-rolled box loses the platform's forced-colours and high-contrast handling, and this menu is exactly where someone goes when something is hard to see.

A mode that changes what the app shows says so beside the control that turns it off (.expert-flag) — the gear looks identical in both states, so without it someone who left expert mode on has no cue why their screen differs. Quiet on purpose: it states a fact, it is not something to act on.

Mixed-script hint

A word carrying a Latin letter where a Cyrillic one belongs (::highlight(sy-typo-script)) — Mати, Toм. Marked apart from the hint above because the two say different things: that one is a suggestion to look again, this one is a defect the checker is certain of and can name. Doubled and orange rather than wavy and red, so the two differ in shape as well as colour.

The offending letter is marked inside the word (::highlight(sy-typo-letter)) — the one place a filled mark earns its keep. T and Т are the same shape, so naming the letter in words shows the reader two glyphs they cannot tell apart; marked where it stands, the eye goes straight to it.

Hovering the word also shows .typo-tip, which says what belonged in its place. The tip floats over the page rather than sitting in the text — the hints never touch the document being edited.

App loader

The boot state of a view: .app-loader centres the app's own mark in the empty screen, breathing, with vibration rings radiating out of it on --aura. It replaced a bare left-aligned "Loading…" line, which read as a glitch rather than as the app waking up.

Half of the component is not CSS. When it may be seen lives in js/boot_loader.js: a show is delayed (LOADER_SHOW_DELAY_MS) so a warm cache never flashes it, and a mark that did appear is held (LOADER_MIN_VISIBLE_MS) so it cannot blink out. Leaving goes through .app-loader--out, and the content that arrives rises in on .sy-enter. Under prefers-reduced-motion the rings stop travelling but stay drawn — they are what says "working" rather than "stuck".

A failed load reuses the same frame with .status-error: the mark goes, the message stays. One home for "not the content yet", so there is no second place to keep in sync.

Sync status

The .sync-cloud chip — one elegant cloud, three tones. Blue progress (two sync arrows), green when synced, red on error. The edit + marker chips both use it; glyphs come from syncCloudSvg(), so this catalog can't drift from the app.

Export menu

Everything a reviewer takes away from a video, behind one icon button in the preview header: the subtitle file, and the video with those subtitles burned in. .export-pop wraps the button and the menu and is the containing block the menu is positioned against — so the menu opens directly beneath the button, floats over the page on --z-dropdown, and moves nothing. The caret is a rotated square, so the menu's own border carries on around it.

It carries no dismiss control: a click anywhere outside the anchor closes it, the button is its toggle, and Escape closes it too. Closing keeps a running render — reopening picks the polling back up.

The video item is the progress readout

The second row is not a button beside a progress panel. It wears one of four faces — create, building… please wait, download, or absent for a session that cannot dispatch — and the render's track, times, run link and error line are that row's own continuation, indented to its text.

The track is four phase segments with visible seams, each as wide as its share of the work (5 / 15 / 70 / 10). The long middle is visibly the long middle: the form itself says "three quick certainties, then one patient stretch". Fills reuse --sync-progress, the same in-progress tone as the sync cloud, and the render segment advances in ten crisp increments as ffmpeg's own progress gates complete.

The meta line is dot-separated and every dot is earned: the separators are ::before on adjacent non-empty spans, so a missing elapsed or remaining time leaves no stray dot. In this narrow a menu the phase takes its own line and the two times share the next. The remaining time is the only estimated wording — faintest AA-passing --fg5, softly worded, and not italic, because it is extrapolated from a measured rate rather than guessed.

A render runs for ~25 minutes, so the component is announced as well as drawn — and it is one control, not four: the track is the role="progressbar" carrying aria-valuenow, the segments are aria-hidden, the phase line alone is a polite live region (the elapsed counter ticks every 5s and must stay out of it), and the error line is role="alert". A failed phase is marked on the segment body, so it shows even at zero fill.
